SkyView

The sky is blue. Until it isn't.
Useful knowledge comes first, complete knowledge comes later.

As children, we're taught that the sky is blue.
but that's not entirely true.

Understanding why the sky appears blue requires learning about light, wavelengths, scattering, molecules, and physics.
But none of that matters when you're five years old.
At that stage, "the sky is blue" is exactly the right answer.

It's simple. It's useful. It's a placeholder for future knowledge that allows us to move forward.

As we learn more, our understanding becomes more detailed and more accurate.
We replace simple explanations with better ones. Not because the original explanation was useless,
but because we've reached the point where we need something deeper.

This site is built around that idea.

You'll find guides, projects, and experiments that focus on providing the basic knowledge and placeholders you need now.
When the time comes to dig deeper, the deeper explanations will be waiting.

Sometimes "the sky is blue" is enough.
The content on this site often favors practical understanding over exhaustive detail.
Many explanations are intentionally simplified so that you can get moving before you know everything. They are meant to be useful, but not comprehensive.

Useful does not always mean complete.
Simple does not always mean inaccurate.

You don't need a complete understanding to take the first step.
